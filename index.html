How the Easy Support algorithm works

The pain with supports isn't building them — it's removing them without gouging the print. Contact strength scales with contact area, so shrinking each tip's footprint makes the whole support peel away with a fingernail.

1
Find overhangs

Every down-facing face steeper than the overhang threshold (45° by default) is flagged.

2
Drop pillars

Points are spread on a grid and ray-cast straight down to land on the model or the plate.

3
Slim the tips

Each pillar tapers from a wide, stable base to a slim contact tip — ½ the diameter → ¼ the area.

4
Export

Model + supports are written back to a printable .3mf (or .stl) you drop into any slicer.

Contact reduction is measured, not assumed — the tool reports the achieved ratio (π·rtip² vs. a standard flat tip) live. Two modes: Generate new easy-remove supports on any file, or Modify existing — pinch the contact band of supports already present as separate objects in a multi-body .3mf.

3D Browser + editor features

  • Formats: .stl · .3mf · .obj · .ply · .gltf/.glb — auto-detected by extension
  • Mouse: left-drag orbit · middle-drag pan · right-drag pan · wheel zoom (touch supported)
  • Z-up print-bed orientation, auto-fit, live bounding-box dimensions in mm
  • Move a face (M): click a flat surface → the view rotates head-on and a surface-aligned 3-axis gizmo appears; drag an axis to push/pull. Two modes (⬆ button): EXTRUDE (default) builds new walls and leaves the rest of the mesh untouched, or STRETCH deforms the attached faces. Refine the selection with Ctrl+drag (add) / Shift+drag (remove) a rectangle — it snaps to strong edges, and stops snapping if you keep moving off one
  • Select an object (O): click a part — a standalone or weakly-attached blob is found by connectivity and chopped at its narrowest cross-section, then dragged off, copied/cut/pasted, or deleted
  • Draw (R): pick shape ▭ rectangle / ◯ circle and recess (CUT) or protrude (ADD), then drag it on a surface — a real boolean applies on release. Corners snap to nearby vertices (with auto-backoff)
  • Inverse (Ctrl+I) makes the negative (block minus shape); Chop (Ctrl+O) is interactive — pick the X/Y/Z plane, type its position, hover the half to keep, Enter for a flush cut
  • Delete a selected face (Del in Move mode) or object; Save the edited mesh as .3mf / .stl
  • Ruler (K): click a start surface then an end point; the X/Y/Z axes align to that surface and the cursor snaps to the nearest axis (or goes free off-axis). Rulers are selectable and deletable
  • Undo/redo (⌘/Ctrl+Z, ⇧Z) and copy / cut / paste / duplicate objects (⌘/Ctrl+C/X/V/D)
  • Opens a G-code toolpath when a sliced .3mf (or .gcode) has no mesh; remembers your last file and reopens it next visit
  • Wireframe, model color, background color, grid & axes toggles, drag-and-drop anywhere
Bundled sample: 2x2_ver1.3mf preloads automatically. Try the ▲ Test overhang built-in model in the Easy Support tool to see supports generate instantly.
